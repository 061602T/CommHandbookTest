# Committee Membership

Committee service is one of the many valuable benefits of City Bar
membership, and there are many members eager to get involved and
contribute. We encourage chairs to welcome new members to their
committees and give them an opportunity to participate, contribute their
ideas, and build meaningful connections. Please keep your committee
roster active and full by filling vacancies as they arise; new members
can be added throughout the year. Staying connected with your members
between meetings and during the summer can also help maintain enthusiasm
and engagement. An active and engaged committee not only strengthen the
City Bar but also creates a rewarding experience for every member who
chooses to serve.

## Committee Member Appointment Process

Committee members are appointed by the President, who acts on the
recommendation of committee chairs. Members of committees generally
serve three-year terms. Though the majority of members are appointed in
the spring and summer to begin a term starting in the fall, committee
vacancies may be filled throughout the year.

Each committee has its own **Committee Management Page**, available to
committee chairs and secretaries. This page is a valuable resource for
managing your committee and staying up to date on membership. From the
Committee Management Page, you can:

-   **Access an up-to-date committee roster** whenever you need it.

-   **See current vacancies** in each membership class.

-   **Review committee applicants**, including their bios, resumes, and
    City Bar member profiles.

-   **Nominate applicants for committee membership** with the click of a
    button. Please note that submitting a nomination does not
    automatically add the applicant to the committee; it simply notifies
    City Bar staff that you would like the member to be considered for
    appointment.

We encourage Committee chairs and secretaries to utilize the Committee
Management Page whenever possible.

To access the **Committee Management Page** you must be a committee
chair or secretary. Please log into your City Bar account and click on
Local Committee Leaders and Members, and you should find the link to the
Committee Management page next to your committee's name.

If you need help navigating the page, please contact Committee
Membership at committeemembership@nycbar.org.

Committee Membership Guidelines

When recommending new members, please keep the following guidelines in
mind:

-   **Voting members:** Each committee may have up to **39 voting
    members**, divided into three classes of 13. There is no limit on
    the number of voting members for Councils, Task Forces, or Working
    Groups.

-   **Non-voting members:** In addition to the 39 voting members,
    committees may include non-voting members, such as affiliates, law
    students, and adjuncts. See the category definitions below for more
    information.

-   **Members from the same firm or organization:** No more than **one
    voting member** from the same firm, law department, organization, or
    institution may serve on a committee. However, a committee may have
    one voting member and additional non-voting affiliate members from
    the same firm or employer, subject to the criteria below.

-   **Reporting changes:** Please report all member resignations and new
    member nominations to Committee Membership at
    committeemembership@nycbar.org or submit them through the Committee
    Management Page.

### Term Limits & Bringing in New Voices
The City Bar has established term limits to help committees maintain a
balance of experience and new perspectives.

-   **Committees:** Voting members serve three-year terms and may not
    serve two consecutive three-year voting terms on the same committee.
    After completing three years as a voting member, a member may be
    appointed to the same committee as a non-voting affiliate member.

-   **Councils, Task Forces, and Working Groups:** Members serve
    three-year voting terms and may be reappointed for a consecutive
    three-year voting term.

These limits are designed to encourage chairs to periodically consider
the composition of their committees and make room for new voices while
retaining experienced members who are actively engaged in the
committee's work.

Automatic reappointment year after year can limit opportunities for new
members and make it more difficult for a committee to bring in fresh
ideas and perspectives. When a member's appointment is not renewed, we
encourage them to explore other committees and other ways to participate
in City Bar activities and to consider reapplying to the committee in a
future year.

If you are having difficulty filling committee vacancies or would like
to discuss ways to attract new members, we are happy to help. Please
reach out to Martha Harris (212.382.6607 or mharris@nycbar.org) to
brainstorm ideas and explore options together.

# Affiliate Members
## What Is an Affiliate Member?
An **Affiliate Member** is a non-voting committee member appointed to
serve for a one-year term. Affiliate Members must be members of the
profession and members of the City Bar.

At the end of the one-year term, an Affiliate Member may be reappointed
at the request of the committee chair.

Affiliate Members are **not included in the committee's limit of 39
voting members**, who serve in three staggered classes.

## When Is an Affiliate Member Appropriate?
Appointing someone as an Affiliate Member may be appropriate in a
variety of circumstances. The examples below are illustrative and are
not intended to be exhaustive. A person who fits one of these
circumstances may also be considered for one of the committee's 39
voting positions.

An Affiliate Member may be appointed to:

-   **Extend the service of a member rotating off the committee.** An
    Affiliate appointment may allow a departing voting member to
    continue contributing to the committee.

-   **Add a member from the same firm or organization as a voting
    member.** A committee may have more than one Affiliate Member from
    the same firm or organization, provided the chair determines that
    the committee's discussions and perspectives will remain balanced.
    However, **only one member of a firm or organization may serve as a
    voting member**.

-   **Engage a junior member of the bar.** An Affiliate appointment can
    provide a junior member with an opportunity to become involved in
    the committee's work and gain experience.

-   **Include a government official or other senior individual.** An
    Affiliate appointment may be appropriate for an individual who
    brings valuable experience or perspective but may not be able to
    make the time commitment required for full committee participation.

-   **Continue the service of a recent outgoing chair.** An Affiliate
    appointment may allow the most recent outgoing chair to remain
    involved when the former chair wishes to continue serving on the
    committee.

Please contact Committee Membership at committeemembership@nycbar.org
with any questions about who qualifies as an Affiliate Member.

**Adjunct Members (Non-Lawyers)**

Committee chairs may appoint non-lawyers to the committee as Adjunct
Members so long as their addition to the committee adds value to the
committee's work, deliberations and perspective. Adjunct Members are
non-voting members and do not count towards the limit of 39 members.
Adjunct Members are appointed for one-year terms and may be reappointed
at the request of the chair.

**Law Student Members**

Chairs are urged to consider appointing law student members to the
committee. Law student members are non-voting members and do not count
against the committee's limit of 39 members. Law student members are
appointed for one-year terms and may be reappointed at the request of
the chair. Many committees find that **having one to five law student
members is an appropriate and workable range**.

# Appointing a Committee Secretary and Role of the Committee Secretary
## Appointing a Committee Secretary
Before the September committee meeting, chairs should appoint a
secretary and notify Committee Membership at
committeemembership@nycbar.org .

The chair may appoint:

-   **A voting committee member** to serve as secretary. The secretary
    will continue to be a voting member of the committee.

-   **An affiliate member** to serve as secretary. The secretary will
    continue to serve as an affiliate member.

-   **Someone from their firm or organization who is not currently a
    committee member.** In this case, the secretary will serve as a
    **non-voting member** of the committee and will need to join the
    City Bar if they are not already a member.

-   From time-to-time chairs find it advantageous to have **two
    committee secretaries which is permittable.**

**Responsibilities of the Committee Secretary**

The secretary is generally responsible for the following administrative
and organizational tasks:

-   **Recording attendance and preparing meeting minutes.**

-   **Distributing draft minutes promptly.** Draft minutes should be
    made available to committee members for review and approval by
    posting them to the committee's City Bar Central Group or
    circulating them by email.

-   **Finalizing and filing approved minutes.** Once approved, the final
    minutes should be emailed to **Nayeli Bautista** at
    NBautista@nycbar.org and posted to the committee's City Bar Central
    Group. Posting the final monthly minutes on City Bar Central helps
    maintain an ongoing record of the committee's discussions and
    activities.

-   **Managing the committee's City Bar Central Group**, including
    keeping committee materials and information organized and up to
    date.

-   **Assisting the chair with administrative and organizational
    matters**, including collecting committee dues assessments and
    coordinating meeting arrangements with Catering & Meeting Services.

-   **Assisting the chair with vetting and nominating members** and
    managing the committee roster.

-   **Liaising with City Bar staff** on the committee's behalf.

# Attendance and Removal of a Committee Member
## Attendance
Committee membership is **individual and not transferable**. A committee
member may not send a substitute to attend a committee meeting on their
behalf. This policy recognizes that committee members are appointed to
participate in their individual capacity and helps protect the
confidentiality of committee discussions and proceedings.

Committee member attendance should be recorded and included with the
meeting minutes.

## Removal for Attendance or Cause
Under **Bylaw 5.2(10)**, a committee member may be removed after **three
unexcused absences during a committee year**. This provision allows the
committee to replace a member who is unable to participate regularly
with someone who can more actively contribute to the committee's work.

Under **Bylaw 5.2(11)**, a committee member or chair may be removed
**for cause**. Committees are expected to maintain a civil and
professional environment that supports thoughtful discussion and healthy
debate. Personal attacks, disruptive or inappropriate conduct, or other
behavior that significantly interferes with the committee's ability to
function or otherwise violates the City Bar's Member & Visitor Conduct
Policy may constitute cause for removal.

# Committee Organization
Whether you are a new or returning chair, it is important to organize
the committee for the new committee year, which begins in September. In
addition to appointing new members, there are several annual
organizational tasks that will help ensure the committee is prepared to
begin the year.

Before or at the first committee meeting, the chair and secretary should
typically:

-   **Identify topics and priorities for the year.** Discuss issues the
    committee would like to address, including potential programs,
    events, or other activities.

-   **Set and confirm the meeting schedule.** Establish meeting dates
    and reserve meeting space as far in advance as possible. Provide
    those dates to members as soon as possible so they can add to their
    calendars.

-   **Determine the catering plan.** Decide what type of food and
    beverage service will be provided at committee meetings throughout
    the year.

-   **Set committee dues.** Determine the amount members will be
    assessed to help cover the cost of food and beverages at meetings.

-   **Begin forming subcommittees.** Identify areas that would benefit
    from smaller working groups and begin establishing subcommittees to
    focus on specific topics, projects, or functions.

# Committee Dues Assessments and Committee Dues Program
## Setting Committee Dues
Each committee should set a dues assessment for its members that is
sufficient to cover the committee's anticipated food and beverage costs
for the committee year. Committee dues may also be useful for things
such as covering the cost of guest speakers or light refreshments at
committee programs and events as well as gifts for members, etc. The
committee is responsible for setting and collecting its own dues.

Typically, the **committee secretary or another person designated by the
chair** will notify members of the assessment, collect the dues, and
keep track of payments.

Committees may want to consider the following when setting their
assessments:

-   **Members who cannot pay the full assessment:** Some committees use
    a two-tier assessment, with a lower assessment for public interest
    and government lawyers.

-   **Law student members:** We recommend that law student members **not
    be charged a dues assessment**.

## City Bar Committee Dues Program
The City Bar offers a **Committee Dues Program** that allows the Finance
Department to manage and track a committee's dues balance.

Under the program:

-   The Finance Department maintains the committee's dues account and
    posts **monthly statements** to the committee's City Bar Central
    page.

-   The committee will continue to receive copies of its catering
    invoices and can review them for accuracy.

-   If there is an issue with a catering invoice, please contact the
    Finance Department at
    **CommDuesProgram@nycbar.org**.

-   If the Finance Department does not receive a dispute within **10
    business days**, the catering invoice will be considered approved
    and charged against the committee's dues balance.

-   Funds in the committee's dues account may also be used for other
    appropriate committee expenses. [**A** **check request form is
    available
    here**.](https://documents.nycbar.org/files/committee_dues_check_request_form.pdf)

# Minutes
Each committee is required to keep minutes of its meetings. The
**committee secretary** is generally responsible for recording
attendance and preparing the minutes.

## Recording Attendance
Attendance should be included with the minutes by listing:

-   **Members present**

-   **Members not present**

There is no need to identify absences as "excused" or "unexcused" in the
minutes.

For purposes of internally tracking attendance, an **excused absence**
generally means that a member has notified the committee that they will
not be able to attend. An **unexcused absence** generally means that a
member has not responded to the meeting notice. This distinction is
important when considering the removal of a committee member for non
participation

## Confidentiality
Committee proceedings are confidential. **All committee minutes should
be marked "CONFIDENTIAL."**

## Distributing and Maintaining Minutes
Once the minutes have been finalized, please send a copy to **Nayeli
Bautista** at NBautista@nycbar.org.

Committees are also strongly encouraged to post final minutes to their
**Committee City Bar Central Page**. By posting minutes on City Bar
Central it:

-   Maintains a central record of the committee's discussions;

-   Ensures that committee members can access the minutes; and

-   Notifies the committee's Board Liaison that the minutes have been
    posted.

Finally, committees are strongly encouraged to **share their minutes
with the leadership of other committees with similar or overlapping
areas of responsibility**. Sharing information can help committees
identify opportunities for collaboration, encourage joint efforts, and
avoid duplicating work.

## Board Liaisons and Committee Clusters
**Committee Clusters**

City Bar committees that focus on substantive areas of law are organized
into **clusters**, such as the Criminal Law Cluster, the Litigation
Cluster, etc. A cluster is a group of committees with overlapping areas
of responsibility or interests in related legal topics.

Clusters are intended to **encourage communication and collaboration**,
not to limit which committees can work together. Committees are
encouraged to collaborate with **all** committees, both those inside and
outside their cluster, whenever their work or interests overlap.

Each cluster is assigned a **Board Liaison** to help support and
coordinate the work of the committees within the cluster.

## Role of the Board Liaison
A Board Liaison can provide guidance and assistance to committees when
appropriate and can help connect the committee's work with the broader
interests and activities of other committees and the City Bar.

If a Board Liaison has been assigned to your committee, the liaison's
name will appear on the committee's membership roster. The liaison
should receive:

-   Meeting notices;

-   Copies of committee minutes;

-   Draft committee reports; and

-   Other relevant committee correspondence and materials.

A Board Liaison **is not a member of the committee and does not vote**
on matters or actions considered by the committee.

Accordingly:

-   The liaison should **not be listed as a committee member** on
    committee reports or other committee products.

-   The liaison should **not be included on committee attendance lists
    in the minutes**.

-   If the liaison attends a committee meeting, they may be listed as a
    **guest**.

## Cluster Meetings and Collaboration
Each fall, the Board Liaison brings together the chairs of the
committees in the cluster. These meetings provide an opportunity for
chairs to meet one another, share information, and identify
opportunities for collaboration.

Committees are encouraged to keep communication open throughout the year
by sharing **minutes and other relevant information** with other
committees in the cluster when appropriate. Sharing information can help
committees coordinate their work, identify opportunities for joint
projects, and avoid duplicating efforts.

## Engaging City Bar Members
Clusters and individual committees are also encouraged to consider ways
to engage other City Bar members who practice in their area of law.
Possible activities include **receptions, networking breakfasts with a
featured speaker, and other educational or networking programs**.

# Annual Report
In the spring or summer, each committee should prepare a brief **annual
report** summarizing its activities during the current committee year
and its plans and priorities for the coming year.

**Every other year, the committee chair meets with the President of the
City Bar** to discuss the committee's work and plans. These meetings
generally take place in late spring and summer. As part of the meeting,
the chair and the President will review the Annual Report and discuss
the committee's activities and upcoming priorities .

The annual report should include:

-   A brief summary of the committee's activities and accomplishments
    during the current committee year;

-   The committee's plans and priorities for the coming year; and

-   Any suggestions regarding the committee's powers, duties,
    membership, structure, or activities.

If the current chair is **rotating off the committee**, the incoming
chair should work with the outgoing chair to develop the committee's
plans and priorities for the coming year and participate in preparing
the annual report, as appropriate.

The annual report is an opportunity for the committee to reflect on its
work, identify priorities for the coming year, and share ideas for how
the committee can best serve the City Bar and its members.

# Committee Meetings
Committee meetings are where ideas take shape and turn into action. Give
members the opportunity to develop and pursue ideas, while setting clear
deadlines for reporting back to the full committee.

Please remind members that all committee meetings and discussions are
confidential and that they should leave their "client hats" at the door.
Committee members are participating in their capacity as City Bar
members, not as representatives of their clients or law firms.

Meetings should include a balance of working sessions and engaging guest
speakers. Committees should also consider holding joint meetings with
other committees to explore topics of mutual interest or to invite
related committees to participate in a guest speaker program.

The City Bar encourages committees to engage new lawyers and introduce
them to the City Bar community. For example, consider hosting a meeting
where each committee member invites a junior colleague or friend to
attend and learn more about the committee and its work.

Committee meetings should provide a civil, professional environment for

## Confidentiality of Meetings

It is important for members to feel comfortable sharing their thoughts
and viewpoints freely. All committee meetings are confidential, the
disclosure of the views of individual members of the committee, or of
positions being considered by the committee, is a violation of the
rights of the committee and committee members, except as noted in the
Committee Reports, Policy Recommendations and Advocacy Section of this
Handbook. Meetings, particularly the portion of the meeting conducting
committee business and discussion, should not be recorded or taped.

## NYS Government Officials at Committee Meetings

New York law strictly bans gifts to New York State government officials
and officials of New York municipalities with populations of 50,000 or
more, with limited exceptions. Providing a meal, or paying meal expenses
of State officials, is banned, with the following relevant exceptions:

-   Complimentary attendance, food and beverage offered by the sponsor
    of a widely attended event. The term "widely attended event" shall
    mean an event: (A) which at least twenty-five individuals other than
    members, officer, or employees from the governmental entity in which
    the public official serves attended or were in good faith invited to
    attend, and (B) which is related to the attendee's duties or
    responsibilities or which allows the public official to perform a
    ceremonial function appropriate to his or her position. For the
    purposes of this exclusion, a public official's duties or
    responsibilities shall include but not be limited to either (1)
    attending an event or a meeting at which a speaker or attendee
    addresses an issue of public interest or concern as a significant
    activity at such an event or meeting; or (2) for elected public
    officials, or their staff attending with or on behalf of such
    elected officials, attending an event or a meeting at which more
    than one-half of the attendees, or persons invited in good faith to
    attend, are residents of the county, district or jurisdiction from
    which the elected public official was elected.

-   Meals or refreshments when participating in a professional or
    educational program and the meals or refreshments are provided to
    all participants.

-   Food or beverage valued at \$15 or less.

Please do not offer or agree to any reimbursement of expenses of New
York State or local government officials. This rule does not apply to
Federal officials. If you have any questions about this law, please
contact **Senior Policy Counsel, Mary Margulis-Ohnuma (212.382.6767 or
mmargulis-ohnuma@nycbar.org)** before inviting a state or local official
to any event.

# Committee Reports, Policy Recommendations and Advocacy

The City Bar provides a professional home for the legal community that
equips and mobilizes a diverse legal profession to practice with
excellence, promotes reform of the law, and upholds the rule of law and
access to justice in support of a fair society and the public interest
in our community, our nation, and throughout the world. One way the City
Bar accomplishes this mission is through the written work product of our
committees, which we refer to as committee "reports" and which includes
such things as comments on pending legislation, letters to public
officials and amicus briefs.

To learn more about the process of developing and issuing a committee
report, please review the following sections, read our [overview on
making public
statements](https://www.nycbar.org/issues-policy/public-statements-of-the-new-york-city-bar-association/)
to get a sense of how and when we speak, visit the Issues & Policy page
on our website, or contact a member of the Policy Department.

## Drafting a Committee Report

**Types of reports**

Any written product drafted by a committee is governed by the City Bar's
report review process, outlined below. This includes, but is not limited
to, amicus briefs, letters to elected officials, legislative reports,
public comments, hearing testimony, policy recommendations, surveys,
FOIL requests, op-eds, questionnaires, etc. *See* the "Pre-Publication
Clearance" section for more information.

**Prior Positions**

Before undertaking a report, committees should check to see if the City
Bar has a prior position on the issue. The Committee Reports section of
our website provides reports from at least the past twenty years and the
Policy Department or Library can check our internal records for older
reports. Committees are encouraged to contact these departments if they
have trouble determining whether the City Bar has issued any reports on
the topics they are considering.

The existence of a prior City Bar report does not preclude the same
committee or a different committee from issuing a subsequent report on
the same topic; however, the subsequent report cannot take a different
position on the issue, absent a compelling reason. Committees can write
additional reports on a topic provided they are updating an old report
with new data, offering a new analysis from a different committee\'s
perspective or doing a deeper study of the issue.

An existing City Bar position can be reevaluated if a significant amount
of new data is presented or there is a change in circumstances that
alters the conditions in which the issue was initially considered. In
such a case, the then-sitting President must approve the change in
position.

*A note on reissuing reports:* Committees are encouraged to update and
reissue their past reports when appropriate, particularly when it comes
to reports on legislation. When advocating for a legislative change, it
is important that committee reports reflect the most recent bill
information and any amendments that may have been made since the
reports\' issuance. Reissued reports do not need to be reapproved by the
committee(s) or President, provided no significant changes are made to
the position and policies discussed; however, discretion is given to the
chair(s) and Policy Department to determine what is the best approach.
If your committee is interested in reissuing a prior report, please
contact the Policy Department.

**Special Considerations with Respect to Amicus Briefs**

There are instances when a court would benefit from additional insights
and expertise beyond that offered by the parties in a case, and we are
pleased that many City Bar committees have taken on the role of
providing such guidance through amicus briefs submitted on a wide range
of topics. As noted above, amicus briefs must go through the same
vetting and approval process as any other City Bar-issued report.

In addition, amicus briefs involve certain other considerations. For
example, although such briefs may purport to offer non-partisan legal or
other arguments, they ultimately may favor one party in a matter over
another. Moreover, such briefs often involve significant and
time-intensive research and drafting work. For these reasons, we advise
that, before submitting a draft amicus brief, the committee submit an
outline of the contemplated brief or summary of the proposed arguments
for review by the Policy Department and approval by the President before
beginning substantive work on the actual brief.

Amicus briefs also are subject to certain requirements that make the use
of an appellate printing service necessary (or at least advisable).
Appellate printers are expensive—and while we want to encourage
committees to file amicus briefs, as a nonprofit organization we do
attempt to keep our costs as low as possible. Thus, as early as possible
in the process (preferably as early as when the committee begins
contemplating an amicus brief), we ask that the committee discuss ways
it can help defray some or all of the costs of the appellate printer.

Cost-saving steps:

-   Discuss among your committee members whether one or some of the law
    firms where they work can cover the appellate printer costs for your
    project (sometimes a single law firm can foot the bill, and other
    times several law firms are willing to split the cost)

-   Contact an appellate printer early in the process to ensure that
    they offer a pro bono or nonprofit discount (many do), and secure
    such discount for your project

-   Discuss timing considerations with the appellate printer (When do
    they need the brief in order to process it in time? Does your brief
    have extensive or complicated exhibits? Will the printer be handling
    filing and service? etc.) to ensure that they do not charge
    additional fees to "expedite" or "rush" your project

-   Create the cover page, Table of Contents and Table of Authorities
    before sending the brief to the printer, rather than having the
    appellate printer generate them. Providing these to the appellate
    printer can significantly reduce costs.

If the committee is unable to come up with a source of funding and needs
to ask the City Bar to pay some or all of the printing costs, please
submit a written request for approval—including a written estimate
from the appellate printer reflecting the pro bono or nonprofit
discount— *before* engaging the appellate printer's services. Please
know that your efforts in these regards help to extend our resources and
further the City Bar's mission and the important work of all our
committees.

**Consultation and Review of Reports with Other Committees**

Committees should feel free to initiate projects without undue
inhibitions caused by possible concurrent jurisdiction with other
committees. However, at an early stage, committees should inform the
chairs of other committees having a possible interest in the subject
being pursued, so that activities can be properly coordinated.
Consulting the other committees in your cluster is usually a good
starting point; for a full list of City Bar committees, click here. The
Policy Department can also help you identify relevant committees to
contact.

A joint report with another committee(s) strengthens the impact of a
report while at the same time adding valuable perspectives to its
substance. By joining forces, or simply consulting in the earliest
stages of serious work efforts, committees may avoid frustrating
publication delays in the event of a disagreement at a later date with
another committee that the President or Board of Directors might have to
resolve. When submitting a report for review, committees should indicate
on their **[Report Intake
Form](https://services.nycbar.org/Members/Upload_Report/Members/Committee_Reports/Submit_Report.aspx?hkey=dfb57984-a173-4e61-a063-67f93c5301e1)**
any other committees consulted during the drafting of the report.

If, upon receiving a report, the Policy Department and/or President
determine that another committee should review it that has not already
done so, the report will be sent to the committee with a request to
provide any comments in writing. Depending on the nature of the report
and any relevant timing considerations, we try to give committees
through their next scheduled committee meeting to send feedback and/or
vote to sign on. Where there is a need for faster action or for more
time, we work with the authoring and reviewing committees to set a
reasonable schedule for feedback and sign-on. If a committee raises
concerns regarding another committee's report, the President will work
with the committees to resolve the matter promptly. If agreement cannot
be reached, the City Bar's By-Laws empower the President to resolve the
issue and permit an appeal of the President's decision to the Board of
Directors (*See* By-Law 5.4.7).

**Content and Formatting of Reports**

While the content and format of committee reports will vary depending on
the topic and goal of the report, all committee work should aim to be
well-balanced, well-supported and thoughtful. All efforts should be made
to ensure any work coming out of the committees meets the high standards
of the City Bar and upholds its mission.

To help with the finalizing of reports, the City Bar has developed a
general Style Guide for committees to use. Please consult this guide
when drafting reports and remember to check (and date) all citations
before submitting. For examples of committee reports, please visit the
Committee Reports section of our website. While we appreciate any
efforts committees can take in the formatting of reports before they are
submitted, please be assured that all reports will ultimately be
reviewed and put in final format by the Policy Department prior to
publishing.

## Confidentiality of Committee Deliberations on Policy Issues

The Board of Directors has adopted the following resolution regarding
confidentiality of City Bar and committee proceedings:

> *"Deliberations at committee meetings are normally understood by the
> committee members to be part of the process of reaching conclusions
> and are not intended for public disclosure of any kind. At the same
> time, the subjects being discussed at committee meetings are
> frequently issues of public concern which are not better resolved in a
> total vacuum. Some exposure to the outside world of the subjects being
> discussed within the committee is likely to result in a better
> conclusion. Committees are free to and are encouraged to seek outside
> views, but such efforts should not be made without the express
> approval of the chair of the committee or of the committee as a whole.
> Moreover, even with such approval, the disclosure of the views of
> individual members of the committee, or of positions being considered
> by the committee, is a violation of the rights of the committee and
> committee members, and is inconsistent with the fact that City Bar
> positions should be publicly announced only in accordance with the
> procedures set out in the by-laws."*

In line with this resolution, committee chairs should periodically
stress to committee members that all conclusions, recommendations or
reports of the committee must remain confidential until approved by the
President for publication. The President requires that all drafts of
reports be clearly marked on the first page with the following language:

*"The following is a draft report, not an approved position of the
Committee or the New York City Bar, and is not intended for distribution
outside the City Bar."*

If the committee wishes to circulate a draft report to agencies or other
persons outside the City Bar for review, please contact the Policy
Department.

## Reaching Decisions on Committee Positions

It is generally undesirable for a committee to take any action of
significance with only a small number of members present or by a vote
that is closely divided (for example, a report should not be advanced to
the President for approval without the affirmative vote of at least one
more than half its voting membership, including the chair(s)). The
Chair(s) is expected to make efforts to avoid such a result whenever
feasible, such as deferring final action or developing alternative
positions that may command greater support on the committee, without any
suggestion, however, that a bare majority lacks power to take final
action.

It is also acceptable to poll committee members not present at the
meeting with regard to their views on a report. In using such a poll,
however, please recognize that some of those responding may not have had
the benefit of a committee discussion of the report; thus, such a poll
should not be used to reverse a decision made at a committee meeting.

Chair(s) may choose to solicit a vote by electronic means, including,
but not limited to, email, conference call or video conferencing.
However, if there is significant dissent or concern raised, chairs
should try not to rely on an email vote if the topic being voted on was
not discussed at a committee meeting.

**Individual Conflicts on Substantive Issues Addressed by Committees**

All members of the City Bar, particularly members of committees, should
take special care to safeguard the City Bar's and its committees'
reputation for integrity and objectivity. In general, it is assumed that
all members leave their clients' hats at the door of the City Bar and
engage in deliberation and debate as individual members of the bar.
Nothing in committee reports can or should be attributed to an
organization, employer or client with which a committee member was or is
affiliated.

However, there may be occasions when a member or members have an
immediate, direct and/or significant interest in a particular issue such
that their involvement in a committee report or action involving the
issue could compromise (or be perceived as compromising) the integrity
of the process. For example, committee members may be personally
involved in litigation, a regulatory proceeding or a lobbying campaign
that directly and significantly relates to the subject matter on which
the committee is developing a position. In such instances, the members
must always disclose the existence of a conflict (or potential conflict)
to the committee, although sensitive and/or confidential information,
including information that may be protected by attorney-client privilege
or other limitations, need not be discussed or disclosed. In some
circumstances, such members may be required to recuse themselves from
discussion, research, drafting and/or approval of any report addressing
the issue.

Whether recusal (and/or abstention) is required should be evaluated on a
case-by-case basis, depending on the nature of the potential
conflict—keeping in mind the dual goals of offering and leveraging our
best experts' knowledge on the issue while taking care to develop policy
positions that reflect balanced debate and deliberation in furtherance
of the City Bar's mission (rather than serve the purpose of favoring any
particular member, organization and/or client). Committee chairs may
determine that the potential conflict does not require the member to be
recused from discussion and/or drafting of the report. However, the
potentially conflicted member should nonetheless abstain from any
vote(s) to approve such report if their participation could be perceived
as attempting to influence the vote to benefit the member, a client, or
an organization with which the member is affiliated.

As part of the report submission process, the committee report intake
form asks whether the committee has read and complied with this section,
and requests the name(s) of any committee member(s) who recused (or
abstained) from any part of the drafting or approval process and whether
such recusal or abstention should be noted in the report (in addition to
noting such recusal or abstention in the committee minutes). Questions
regarding the application of this policy may be directed to the Policy
Department.

Committees also should be mindful of Rule 6.4 of the New York Rules of
Professional Conduct:

> *"A lawyer may serve as a director, officer or member of an
> organization involved in reform of the law or its administration,
> notwithstanding that the reform may affect the interests of a client
> of the lawyer. When the lawyer knows that the interests of a client
> may be materially benefitted by a decision in which the lawyer
> actively participates, the lawyer shall disclose that fact to the
> organization, but need not identify the client. In determining the
> nature and scope of participation in such activities, a lawyer should
> be mindful of obligations to clients under other Rules, particularly
> 1.7."*
>
> *\[Note: Rule 1.7 addresses conflicts of interest involving current
> clients.\]*

## Working With Other Organizations - Issuing Reports and Joining Coalitions

The City Bar is often asked to support issues by joining coalitions or
signing on to the work of other organizations, such as reports, letters,
amicus briefs, petitions, etc. In general, the City Bar does not sign on
to work prepared by another entity or formally join
coalitions/campaigns. By signing on to the work of another group, the
City Bar cedes the use of its name to someone else and limits the
President's authority under its By- Laws to approve positions and
decisions made related to a position, both strategic and substantive.
However, there are some exceptions, outlined below. Please contact the
Policy Department to discuss these exceptions further.

**Joining a Coalition**

While the City Bar cannot formally join coalitions/campaigns, that does
not preclude us from working in tandem with a coalition/campaign. As
part of our advocacy work after the City Bar has an approved position on
an issue, the Director of Advocacy will often reach out to other
organizations/coalitions/campaigns to share the City Bar's work and
learn about their advocacy. This helps us tailor our advocacy strategy
and maximize our impact. Where appropriate, the City Bar and its members
can amplify the work of a campaign/coalition through their networks and
social media. The Director of Advocacy will also share advocacy alerts /
action items and Lobby Day opportunities with committees that are being
hosted by campaigns/coalitions where appropriate.

**Sign on Letters / Form Letters / Signing on to the Work of other
Organizations**

Committees cannot substantially adapt another organization's document
and then issue it as a City Bar report, even if the other organization
requests it and the committee agrees with the position being taken. In
those circumstances, a committee should write an independent report that
reflects the deliberations of the committee and contains appropriate
attribution to the work product of the other organization.

If, after conducting an independent review of an issue, a committee
simply wants to add support to another organization's position, it
should write a letter acknowledging the other organization's position
and include a brief explanation of why the committee supports it. If it
is determined that a form letter/report is the best approach for
advocacy purposes, the letter/report must acknowledge that it is adapted
from another organization's form letter/report and identify the other
organization.

A committee may jointly issue a report with another organization if the
committee is substantively involved in the drafting of the report and it
is approved by the committee and the City Bar President. Joint reports
should reflect the authorship and, if possible, the logos of all
involved organizations.

Notwithstanding the above, there are rare occasions where it is
advisable, as a strategic matter, to join other organizations in making
a statement of significance to the profession or to a particular
practice area, even if the committee does not have an opportunity to
participate in the drafting of the statement. Signing on to a joint
statement under these circumstances requires that the City Bar already
have taken a position consistent with the position being taken in the
statement, and further requires the approval of the President. If you
believe you fall under this set of circumstances, or if you believe
there is a compelling reason for the City Bar to sign onto an
organizational statement for some other reason, please contact the
Policy Department to discuss and seek the President\'s approval.

Please note that while the City Bar is limited in its ability to sign on
to the work of other organizations, it does not preclude committees from
participating in advocacy with other organizations for approved City Bar
positions. Please see the section Report Distribution, Promotion and
Advocacy for more information.

## Finalizing and Required Pre-Publication Clearance of Reports

By-Law 5.4 governs the procedures for approval of reports, conclusions
or recommendations. Prior to release of any report or any written or
oral communication that presents the committee's conclusions or
recommendations on a substantive matter, the report, conclusions or
recommendations must be approved by the President.

Please remember, if a committee wishes to circulate a draft report to
agencies or other persons outside the City Bar for review that too must
first be approved by the President.

When a report has been approved by a committee, the report should be
submitted in Word format for review through our online "Report Intake
Form". This form allows committees to provide some background
information on their work and upload a report for consideration. The
information provided will help with the review, finalization and
distribution of reports.

Submitted reports will be assessed by the Policy Department, which will
coordinate any required review by other committees and by the President.
As part of this process, the Chair(s) may be asked whether committee
deliberations yielded any significant dissent or included any discussion
of known opposing views and how such views were handled. If the
President has questions about or wants changes to be made to the report,
they will be sent to the committee. If the President disapproves a
report, committees are permitted to appeal of the decision to the Board
of Directors (*See* By-Law 5.4). Reports approved by the President are
considered official positions of the City Bar.

## Report Distribution, Promotion and Advocacy

Committees put significant time and resources towards crafting their
reports and it is important that consideration also be given to how
those reports will be conveyed to appropriate stakeholders in order to
maximize their impact. The City Bar has various resources that can help
with the distribution, promotion and advocacy of committee reports and
positions.

**Distribution**

We encourage wide dissemination of committee reports. Approved reports
will be posted on the City Bar's website, provided there is no reason to
refrain from posting. We encourage committees to share final reports
with their professional networks and through social media to enhance the
profile and visibility of the report. See our Social Media Policy for
more information.

The Policy Department reviews all reports and, as appropriate, will work
with committees to prepare a targeted distribution of their report. They
will coordinate distribution with the committee to ensure the report
gets to the right people at the most impactful times. While most
committees know who the appropriate audience should be, we can also help
to identify key legislators, public officials and other bar associations
or organizations that may have an interest in your recommendations. Any
committee recommendations or requests related to distribution can be
noted on the Report Intake Form when submitting a report for review.
Committees are encouraged to consult with the Policy Department before
distributing any reports, particularly in cases where the committee
would like to engage in ongoing advocacy or when the committee is
proposing legislation, so that we can coordinate efforts.

If a committee is filing an amicus brief or submitting a report for
publication in a journal, magazine, etc., submission should be
coordinated with the Policy Department and a copy of the brief or report
as filed should be provided for our records and posting on the City
Bar's website.

Please note that the City Bar holds the copyright on any committee work
product and the right of first refusal on publication. That said, the
City Bar understands the value in disseminating our committees' work
product and supports sharing our members' expertise with the legal
profession and the wider public. If an outside publication wishes to
reprint or otherwise publish a committee report, please contact the City
Bar's general counsel for permission to reprint.

**Advocacy Following Release of a Report**

After issuing a report, one of the ways committees can stay involved and
advance their policy recommendations is through advocacy. For those
committees that want to engage in advocacy work above and beyond the
issuance of a report, the Policy Department can help. The City Bar's
ability to advocate for committee positions is directly tied to the
level of involvement from committee members, so we strongly encourage
committees to reach out to the Policy Department early in their report
deliberations to coordinate efforts. Click here for a brief guide on
steps committees should take following the issuance of a report.

In partnership with committee members, we can help with advocacy in a
number of ways, including:

-   working with committees to determine the appropriate strategy and
    timing of report distribution and helping to develop an advocacy
    plan (which may include utilizing the resources in our
    Communications Department to publicize a report or putting on a
    program to educate the public about the issue);

-   assisting in communicating committee views to policymakers and
    related stakeholders;

-   tracking developments related to legislation or an issue of interest
    and providing updates;

-   arranging meetings or lobby visits with individuals and
    organizations who would be responsible for implementing report
    recommendations;

-   partnering with other bar associations or outside organizations to
    promote our positions and collaborate on advocacy efforts;

-   convening a roundtable of related stakeholders to fully air an issue
    and develop a plan of action; and

-   working with committees to seek sponsors and advocate for
    legislation proposed by committees.

Our website provides a number of resources for committees interested in
undertaking advocacy, such as best practices, tips for tracking
legislation and ways to get involved in advocacy. Committees can also
use our website and social media sites to receive news updates relevant
to the City Bar's policy work, learn when reports are posted on the web,
and get updates on the status of bills they've commented on.

For more information, please contact the City Bar's Director of
Advocacy.

Committee Programs

Organizing a program through the City Bar is another way to promote the
ideas and issues in committee reports. Committees should consider
planning CLE courses or educational events that highlight the topics in
their reports. Creating a public forum where experts, advocates and
other interested parties can have an open dialogue on an issue or newly
enacted legislation provides an excellent opportunity to educate the
profession and public and create interest in the topic. If you are
interested in hosting an event at the City Bar, see the Programs section
of the Handbook.

## Publicity/Media Relations

Many reports are appropriate subjects for publicity. Committees should
work with the Communications Department to develop a strategy to draw
media attention. To ensure the best possible coverage for reports and
recommendations, please fill out all sections of the Report Intake Form
and provide the Communications Department with the names of reporters
and publications that you may wish to alert (Communications will also
utilize the City Bar's media contacts and resources). Committees hoping
for significant media coverage should contact Communications early in
the drafting stages to optimize later press coverage.

Committees are encouraged to prepare op-ed pieces (up to 750 words) and
letters to the editor (up to 250 words) to draw further attention to
reports. As with any committee report, op-ed pieces or letters to the
editor must be approved by the President, even when writing about
approved City Bar positions. In order to provide maximum assistance and
coordination, press releases, op-ed pieces and letters to the editor
will be released only from the Communications Department or with its
supervision. If a committee chair or member is writing an article or
op-ed piece in her/his own name and not in their capacity as chair or
member, they should not identify an affiliation with the City Bar, so as
to avoid confusion as to whether the piece is presenting the City Bar's
positions.

You can also work with the Communications Department to create a podcast
that highlights the topic of your report. Similar to hosting an event,
podcasts provide a flexible and unique format for showcasing an issue.

The City Bar frequently receives calls from the media asking for
background information or for City Bar positions on a wide variety of
legal issues. These calls are handled through the Communications
Department and are an excellent opportunity to call attention to a
committee's work on an issue. Committee chairs, or their designees, are
encouraged to be available to the press not only to discuss committee
reports, but also to advise reporters on subjects within the committee's
expertise. Please try to answer these requests in a timely manner, as
deadlines are typically tight. If for some reason chairs are unable to
handle the particular issue, they should refer it to someone on their
committee who can.

When speaking with the media, committee members should be aware of their
role as a spokesperson. If discussing a committee report or commenting
on an issue about which the City Bar has an official position, members
should be represented as "Chair or member of the New York City Bar's
Committee on \...\...\..." If a committee member is providing general
information, they also can be identified with the City Bar. However, if
a member is giving an opinion on an issue about which the City Bar does
not have an official position, they should make clear with the reporter
that they are speaking as an individual and not representing the City
Bar. They may be identified as "Chair of the City Bar's \_\_\_\_\_
Committee," as that is a fact that speaks to their expertise.

## Tips and Best Practices for Speaking/Presenting on a Virtual Platform

**Video and Audio**

-   Have all devices fully charged and have an electrical socket and
    cable nearby should your battery die and you need to plug in.

-   Make sure you have a good internet connection so you don't drop-off.
    If you can, connect to the internet via an internet cable rather
    than by Wi-Fi. Zoom works on wireless connection but the quality is
    best on a wired internet connection

-   Test your video and audio before your meeting at zoom.us/test.

-   Try to use a headphone or earphones to prevent echoes and feedback.

-   If possible, try to use a good quality camera and headset instead of
    your computer's built-in ones. Zoom works fine with the built-ins,
    but the quality is even sharper with higher quality hardware.

-   Mute your mic when you aren't speaking to prevent/reduce background
    noise.

**Your Image and Environment**

-   **Set up your computer in a quiet place with good lighting**

-   Unless your appearance or background is very inappropriate or
    distracting, turn ON your video. Video is crucial in creating an
    engaging virtual communication.

-   Have a simple background (a plain wall, a potted plant, or a
    bookshelf works perfectly). Zoom also provides virtual backgrounds
    if you need to disguise your environment.

-   Try to be in a room where people won't have to enter during the
    program and alert those you live with that you will be on a Zoom
    Webinar so they don't disturb you.

-   Position yourself so that most of the light is coming from in front
    of you (behind your monitor), instead of behind you. If you have a
    window behind you, shut the blinds. Otherwise, you will be back lit.

-   Try to center your head in the center of the frame.  Adjust your
    camera if it is too low or high. Your camera should be at eye level.
    To achieve this you can also prop your computer up on books or
    adjust your seat.

-   Don't get too close. Position yourself so the camera is seeing you
    from the chest or waist up, instead of just seeing your face. This
    is more natural for the viewer. This is especially beneficial if you
    tend to gesture a lot.

-   Look at the camera when you are speaking. 

**Engage Your Audience**

-   Consider using engagement tools available on Zoom such as polls,
    share screen and Q&A.

-   Share your screen, show PowerPoints, Websites and other documents.

-   Send out a poll. Please send your poll questions to us in advance of
    the program so they can be set-up.

-   Solicit feedback or invite questions via the Q&A function.

# CLE Programs
## CLE Program Approval
The City Bar encourages committees to develop **Continuing Legal
Education (CLE)** programs. Programs can include basic 101 on a
particular topic, introductions to new laws, rules or regulations or
substantive topics, updates for experienced practitioners, or hands-on
training to build legal skills.

Because CLE is required for New York attorneys, programs offering CLE
credit are an important member benefit. **Any program offering CLE
credit must be planned and approved in coordination with the Program
Department.**

### Planning a CLE Program
When your committee has an idea for a CLE program:

1.  **Contact the Program Department.** Share your idea and discuss
    whether CLE credit is appropriate for the program.

2.  **[Complete the CLE Proposal
    Form](https://services.nycbar.org/Members/CLE_Program_Questionnaire/Members/CLE/Upload_Program_Questionnaire.aspx?hkey=af7e04b1-c1f4-4a69-9e6a-1956f5bfb12f).**
    Program staff will review the proposal and confirm whether it is
    approved as a City Bar CLE program. In some cases, a program may be
    better suited to run without CLE credit.

3.  **Choose a date.** Once approved, the Program Department will work
    with you to schedule the program. CLE programs are generally
    scheduled **4--6 months in advance**, while conferences are
    typically scheduled **about one year in advance**. The City Bar also
    tries to accommodate timely or emerging topics on a shorter timeline
    when possible.

4.  The Program staff will **advise you about similar programs** offered
    recently or planned for the future and help determine if there are
    other committees that should be asked to **cosponsor.**

5.  **Identify speakers and develop the program.** Confirm proposed
    speakers and panelists and develop the agenda and schedule as early
    as possible. This gives the Program Department enough time to
    effectively promote the program and make the necessary arrangements.

6.  **Work with City Bar staff.** The Program Department will work with
    the committee to refine the agenda, collect written materials from
    speakers, identify the target audience, and provide **specific
    deadlines and additional instructions**.

### What the Program Department Handles
The Program Department coordinates the logistics and administration of
the CLE program, including:

-   Reserving meeting space and catering.

-   Promoting and announcing the program.

-   Collecting and editing speaker materials.

-   Managing registration and evaluations.

-   Determining CLE credit and distributing CLE certificates.

-   Filing required information with the New York State CLE Board.

-   Maintaining program records as required by CLE rules.

## Written Materials for CLE programs

The Regulations and Guidelines of the State's CLE Board require that:

Written materials must be prepared or compiled specifically for the

suffice. The Program Office reserves the right to edit, delete and
supplement the materials, subject to consultation with the committee.
